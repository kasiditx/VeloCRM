# Product

## Register

product

## Users

VeloCRM serves small and medium business owners and sales teams who need a practical CRM they can install, understand, and use without a dedicated developer. Users are typically managing leads, follow-ups, proposals, invoices, and customer records during normal business operations, often switching between Thai and English.

## Product Purpose

VeloCRM exists to give SME teams a complete sales and customer management workflow in one shared-hosting friendly Laravel product. Success means a buyer can install the system, add leads, convert customers, create proposals and invoices, assign tasks, track activity, and review reports with minimal training.

## Brand Personality

Professional, easy to use, fast.

The product should feel business-ready and trustworthy without feeling heavy or enterprise-only. It should help users move quickly through daily CRM tasks, with clear labels, predictable navigation, and straightforward Thai and English language support.

## Anti-references

VeloCRM should not look or feel like a Laravel default starter app. Avoid generic scaffolded pages, developer-centric language, placeholder branding, and UI copy that mixes Thai and English without purpose.

It should also avoid bloated enterprise CRM patterns: overloaded screens, unclear terminology, excessive configuration before value, and layouts that make small teams feel like they are operating software built for a much larger company.

## Design Principles

1. Make the next action obvious. Each screen should clearly show what the user can do next, especially create, follow up, convert, invoice, and report.
2. Keep CRM language practical. Use business terms that SME owners and sales teams understand, especially in Thai.
3. Prioritize speed of daily work. Navigation, forms, lists, search, and status changes should reduce repeated effort.
4. Preserve confidence in business records. Customer, lead, invoice, proposal, task, note, and attachment data should feel organized and recoverable.
5. Support bilingual use without friction. Thai and English should both read naturally, with no awkward machine-like labels in primary workflows.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.1 AA for core workflows. Keep contrast strong in both light and dark modes, avoid relying on color alone for status, support keyboard navigation for common actions, and keep motion subtle enough for repeated business use. Thai text must have enough space in buttons, tables, navigation, and form labels so translated strings do not feel cramped.
